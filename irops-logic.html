MAG IROPS Detection Logic

How a Malaysia Airlines booking is judged truly disrupted, how each cancelled flight is paired with its replacement, and what the passenger is entitled to as a result. This is the authoritative description of the logic now running in the sandbox.

01

Data source

Every conclusion in this document is derived from one call. No other source is consulted, and nothing is inferred from the customer's own description of what happened.

EndpointGET /api/rest/flight-booking/v1/bookings/{pnr}?lastName={lastName}
Hostsandbox-api.malaysiaairlines.com
AuthOcp-Apim-Subscription-Key header
Ada web action68e76ec39644634ce6b970df — “Retrieve customer booking details”

Two branches of the response carry everything the logic needs:

  • data.airBounds[].flights[] — every flight ever attached to the booking, including dead segments.
  • data.passengers[].segments[].flightId — the flights each passenger currently holds.
Do not use as a disruption signal

data.status remains "confirmed" even when flights on the booking are cancelled. Across all six test PNRs — four of which contain cancellations — it never once changed. Branching on it will silently classify every disrupted booking as healthy.

02

Status codes

Each flight carries a statusCode and a coarser flightStatus. The statusCode is the field to branch on; the API returns its own glossary at dictionaries.statusCode.

CodeAPI nameMeaning in this logic
UN Unable, does not operate Cancelled. The flight will not fly.
TK Confirmed, timechange Ambiguous by itself. Either a genuine retime, or the replacement issued for a cancelled flight. Which one it is cannot be read off the code — see §04.
HK Holding confirmed Confirmed, untouched.
KK Confirmed Confirmed, untouched.
Observed in dictionaries.statusCode across the six sandbox PNRs.
Why TK cannot be counted naively

Treating every TK as a retime double-counts every reallocation: when a cancelled flight is replaced, the replacement is itself a TK. A booking with one cancellation and one replacement would be reported as two disruptions — a cancellation and a phantom retime.

03

The live itinerary

This is the single most important distinction in the logic, and the one that is easiest to get wrong.

airBounds is a history. It retains bounds the passenger no longer travels on. passengers[].segments is the live itinerary — what the passenger actually holds right now. When a cancelled flight is reallocated, the UN flight is dropped from the passenger's segments while remaining in airBounds.

That difference gives an exact test for reallocation:

UN still in segments

Stranded

Nothing was arranged. The passenger holds a flight that will not operate, and is refund-eligible.

UN absent from segments

Reallocated

The passenger was moved. Their replacement is the live TK on the same origin → destination pair.

Worked example — PNR D8AS2C

This booking contains both shapes at once, which is why route-matching alone cannot resolve it. Both legs contain a UN; only one of them is genuinely stranded.

flightIdFlightRouteStatusIn airBoundsIn segmentsConclusion
ST1MH1208KUL → AOR UNyesno Reallocated to MH1252
ST3MH1252KUL → AOR TKyesyes Is the replacement — not a retime
ST2MH1209AOR → KUL UNyesyes Stranded — refund-eligible
The passenger holds exactly ST3 and ST2. ST1 is history.
Corollary

“Itinerary status” must always be read from segments, never from airBounds. Two of the six test PNRs (D87LB2, D8BO6B) contain a UN in airBounds that is absent from the live itinerary. Reading airBounds would wrongly downgrade both to refund-only.

04

Classifying a flight

The resolution runs in a fixed order, per passenger. Order matters: pairing must consume its replacement before retimes are counted, or the replacement is mistaken for an independent retime.

  1. Stranded cancellations first

    Any UN the passenger still holds is cancel_no_transfer. Nothing was arranged for them.

  2. Pair the dropped cancellations

    For each UN the passenger no longer holds, find a live TK they do hold on the same origin and destination; the nearest departure time wins. That TK is marked consumed. Result: cancel_with_transfer, or reroute_other_airline when the replacement's operating.airlineCode is not MH.

  3. Whatever TK is left is a real retime

    A held TK that no cancellation claimed is retime — or delay if its flight number ends in D.

Truly disrupted is then simply: the passenger has at least one classified event. A booking of only HK and KK produces none.

05

Entitlement

What the customer may do is derived from whether they hold a flight at a new time — not from the disruption type, and not from how much notice they were given. A TK in the live itinerary grants both actions, whether that TK is a retime of their own flight or the replacement issued for a cancellation. Cancellations with no TK anywhere leave a refund as the only option, because nothing was arranged and there is no new itinerary to change.

Booking conditionEntitlementOffered
Live itinerary holds a TK — a retime, or a replacement for a cancellation refund_and_flight_change Change my flight · Request a refund · Not at the moment
Cancelled with no TK anywhere — nothing arranged, no new itinerary refund_only Request a refund · Do it later
No disruption at all — HK / KK only none Not disrupted — itinerary plus a Manage My Booking link for extras and SSR
Passengers differ mixed → human agent

Entitlement is a property of the whole booking, not of an individual flight. A booking that mixes a reallocated cancellation with a stranded one — D8AS2C — is entitled to both actions, because a TK exists somewhere on it. The stranded leg is still shown explicitly as having no alternative, so the customer can see why a refund is on the table.

Why a retime grants a flight change

A retime reallocates nothing, so a stricter reading — “no reallocation, refund only” — would leave a retimed passenger unable to move themselves. That reading was tried and reverted: the MAG matrix lists Retime / TK as Refund-or-Flight-Change in both the Advance and IROPS 47–23 h blocks, so a retime carries the full entitlement.

refund_only is therefore reserved for the genuinely stranded case: every disrupted flight cancelled, nothing arranged, no TK in the itinerary. An itinerary of UN + HK — one leg cancelled outright, another untouched — also lands here.

Manage My Booking is blocked while unresolved

For any disrupted booking the customer is told, as its own closing paragraph, that they cannot make changes in Manage My Booking until the disruption is resolved — so it is better resolved in the conversation.

06

Multiple passengers

Classification runs per passenger and is never unioned across the booking. Passengers can be reaccommodated separately, and a union produces two distinct errors at once.

Consider four passengers on a cancelled MH003 where three are moved to MH001 and one is left behind:

ApproachMH003MH001Reported entitlement
Union of all segments stranded phantom retime Single entitlement — the split is invisible
Per passenger Reallocated for 3 · stranded for 1 Correctly consumed as the replacement mixed → agent

When passengers disagree, entitlement resolves to mixed and the conversation goes to a human rather than showing one button set that is wrong for somebody on the booking. Each disruption event also carries the passengers it affects, so an agent can see who needs what.

Not exercised by sandbox data

Only D8BO6B carries multiple passengers (4), and all four hold identical segments. The split-reaccommodation path is covered by synthetic tests only — no sandbox PNR reproduces it.

07

Notice windows

Time-to-departure is still computed and exposed, but under the current entitlement rules it is informational — it no longer decides what the customer may do.

WindowTime to departure
advanceMore than 48 h
irops_24_to_48h24 h to 48 h
irops_within_24hUnder 24 h
departedDeparture already passed
unknownClock unavailable or date unparseable
Divergence from the MAG matrix

The original disruption matrix makes cancel with no transfer refund-only above 24 h but refund or flight change within 24 h. The status rule has no such carve-out: with no TK in the itinerary the customer is refund-only regardless of notice. This is the one place where the implemented logic and the matrix still disagree, and it needs an owner's decision.

Two further matrix rows are only partially represented:

  • Retime, same flight number, next day — “Not Allowed”. Detected when a UN is replaced by a TK carrying the same flight number on a later date, and surfaced as not_allowed_flights. It does not currently suppress the buttons, because the newer rules do not mention the restriction.
  • Delay in CM / “no change”. No field in the response distinguishes this from an untouched booking, so it cannot be detected.

General instructions

The playbook carries cross-cutting rules so that follow-up questions asked after the summary stay consistent with it: state each flight's facts accurately, never describe a retime as a cancellation, never invent a flight, never reveal a status code, honour a stated choice instead of re-asking — and, decisively, never offer a flight change on a booking that is entitled to a refund only.

Display steps receive finished data

The send steps do no analysis. Pairing, entitlement and grouping are all decided in code and handed over as variables; the step's only job is formatting. Both lists a message can print — disruption_flights and disruption_confirmed_flights — are explicit variables, because a step will quietly omit or invent flights it has to infer from context.

10

Platform constraints

Four behaviours of this instance shaped the implementation and will shape anyone else's. Each was diagnosed the hard way.

json and re do not exist

The code-action sandbox provides neither, despite the adacli documentation listing both as pre-provided. The literal parser and the ISO-8601 parser are hand-rolled on string operations. datetime is probed defensively and the logic degrades to unknown windows without it.

Variables arrive as Python repr

Variable-bound inputs are delivered as strings using single quotes and True / None — not JSON. Declaring an input as list fails coercion outright with “not a valid list”. Inputs are declared text and parsed internally; the parser accepts both dialects.

No dry-run, no update

code-actions test returns 405, so snippets cannot be dry-run server-side. PUT is also 405, so every change is a delete-and-recreate, which mints a new action ID and requires re-pointing the playbook.

A failed run step aborts the playbook

When the code action errored, the playbook exited and the reasoner independently chose the self-reaccommodation handoff — dropping the customer into the tool with no context. The mitigation is a code action that cannot fail: it returns a valid, conservative result for empty, truncated and malformed input alike.

Also worth knowing

A competing playbook, [TEST v2] Booking Inquiry, wins on generic phrasing such as “check my booking”. Disruption-specific wording is needed to route into this playbook during testing — and the overlap should be resolved before production.

11

Known gaps

Recorded so they are not rediscovered, roughly in order of how much they matter.

  1. The reallocation rule is an inference

    That a reallocated UN is dropped from passengers[].segments reproduces all eight spreadsheet rows and is the only signal separating D8AS2C's two legs — but it is undocumented behaviour observed across six sandbox PNRs. It should be confirmed with the owner of the booking API, since it is now the load-bearing condition.

  2. No sandbox PNR exercises refund-only

    Every one of the six test PNRs holds a TK, so all six resolve to refund-and-change. The refund_only branch — its two-button prompt and its “no alternative was arranged” wording — is covered by unit tests only. A PNR whose flights are all cancelled with nothing rebooked would close this.

  3. Can the self-reaccommodation tool rebook a stranded leg?

    On a booking with a reallocation elsewhere, the agent will tell a customer their stranded leg is eligible for a refund or a flight change, because entitlement is booking-level. Whether the tool can actually rebook a leg with no alternative is a property of SREAC that the booking data cannot answer. If it cannot, entitlement needs a per-flight qualifier.

  4. Entitlement contradicts the notice-window matrix

    See §07. Within 24 hours the matrix grants a flight change on a no-transfer cancellation; the status-composition rule does not.

  5. Refund and flight change hit the same URL

    Both buttons redirect to the same self-reaccommodation link. If the tool can deep-link to a specific intent, the parameter name is unknown and was not invented.

  6. Original schedules are never returned

    The response carries only current times, so a retime's magnitude is unknowable and “same flight number, next day” is detectable only in the UN + TK shape. Whether MAG ever represents that move as a bare TK is unconfirmed.

  7. Speak-to-an-agent is no longer a button

    The newer button sets do not include it, so it was dropped from the disrupted branches. Customers can still reach a human by asking, and mixed routes there automatically — but the explicit option is gone.
